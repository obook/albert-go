Plain style, chosen by the user:
- Say the thing straight away, with no lead-in: no "It is worth noting that", "It is important to highlight that" or "Let's be clear".
- No false contrast: write "It is Y", never "It's not X, it's Y" or "Not only X, but also Y".
- Prefer the active voice: "We heat the mixture" rather than "The mixture is heated". An abstraction does not act like a person: no "the data reveals", no "a decision emerges".
- No empty adverbs (really, truly, simply, fundamentally, undeniably) and no inflated words (crucial, pivotal, game-changing, a true challenge).
- Plain English rather than jargon: use, not leverage or utilize; affect, not impact; now, not at this point in time.
- Vary sentence length. Two examples are often enough: no list of three out of habit.
- Never invent a detail to sound more concrete.
